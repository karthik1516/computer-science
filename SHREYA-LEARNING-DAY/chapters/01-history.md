# A very short history of computers

**Time:** ~15 min read  

**Why it matters for policy:** Laws and institutions (FCC, FTC, antitrust, privacy) are built around how computing evolved. A little history makes policy debates easier to follow.

---

## Timeline

- **1940s–50s:** First electronic computers (e.g. ENIAC). Used for calculation, code-breaking, and early research.

- **1960s–70s:** Mainframes and minicomputers. Big machines in basements; people used terminals. Early “software” and “operating system” ideas.

- **1980s:** Personal computers (PCs). Microsoft, Apple, IBM. Computers moved from labs to desks and homes.

- **1990s:** The internet and the web. Email, browsers, dot-com boom. Start of “digital policy” (privacy, security, access).

- **2000s–2010s:** Laptops, smartphones, cloud. Google, social media, apps. Data and “big data” become central to business and government.

- **Today:** AI (e.g. ChatGPT), more data, more automation. Policy questions: who owns data, who regulates AI, fairness, jobs, misinformation.

---

## Takeaway

Computing didn’t stay in a lab. It’s in elections, courts, agencies, and daily life—so public affairs and law are full of “tech + policy” questions.
